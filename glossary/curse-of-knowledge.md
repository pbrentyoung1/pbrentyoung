The communication gap created when people who know something can no longer easily imagine what it feels like not to know it.

Experts hear the whole song while everyone else hears taps on a table. The details, context, and reasons feel obvious to the person creating the message because they are already carrying what the audience has not been given.

## In business

The Curse of Knowledge produces jargon, skipped steps, unexplained assumptions, and messages written from inside the organization instead of from the audience's point of view.

## In the church

Church language often assumes familiarity with Scripture, ministries, buildings, rhythms, and unwritten expectations. Caring communication remembers what it felt like to be new and supplies the context insiders no longer notice.

## What remains true

What feels obvious to the sender may still sound like random knocks to the listener.
