# Publishing Action Plan

Last updated: 2026-07-30

## Purpose

This plan turns the editorial and distribution strategy into a sequence of
work.

It answers:

- What to do with the content that already exists
- What to write and develop next
- What to publish through Substack and social media each week
- How the work supports resource development, community building, and
  sustainability

Use `DISTRIBUTION_STRATEGY.md` for the durable channel rules, goals, and
measurement model. Use this document for the current campaign sequence and
action list.

## Current Position

The site already has 15 essays in its published index and one unlisted John 4
series introduction.

This is enough source material to launch a disciplined publishing and
distribution rhythm without rushing new articles.

The immediate opportunity is to:

- Curate the existing work into a clear sequence
- Give each essay an editorial readiness check
- Adapt one idea each week for Substack and social media
- Combine related ideas into useful resources
- Listen to readers before deciding which resources need greater depth
- Preserve the strongest ideas for *Evangelistic Marketing*

Do not import the entire archive into Substack at once. A curated weekly
sequence gives each idea room to travel and gives Brent time to build a
relationship with readers.

## The Three Campaigns

These are ongoing campaigns, not separate brands.

### Resource Development

Purpose: turn Brent's body of thought into practical tools that help churches
and leaders apply the ideas.

The first resource should be a beta **Church Communications Planning Starter
Kit** built from existing material.

Potential components:

- The four First Principle questions
- A one-job worksheet for each deliverable
- Campaign, Project, Deliverable, and Task planning sheet
- A channel mix planner
- A simple creative brief
- A weekly communication review

Later resources may include:

- Story Stewardship Audit
- Brand Decision Scorecard
- Future Congregation Listening Guide
- Touchpoint and Journey Map
- *Evangelistic Marketing* discussion guide
- Workshop and teaching materials

Resource development follows this path:

```text
Essay
    ↓
Reader question
    ↓
Worksheet or framework
    ↓
Small beta test
    ↓
Revised resource
    ↓
Free tool, workshop, product, or book material
```

### Community Building

Purpose: form an ongoing group of readers and practitioners who learn with
Brent, respond to the ideas, and help one another communicate more faithfully.

Community begins as a pattern of relationship, not a new platform.

For the first 90 days:

- Invite replies to each Substack edition.
- Ask one useful question connected to the week's idea.
- Respond thoughtfully to substantive comments and messages.
- Share selected reader questions in later editions with permission.
- Invite a small group to test the first resource.
- Use Substack Notes to participate in relevant conversations, not only to
  promote Brent's work.

Do not create a separate Facebook group, Slack, Discord, or membership platform
until repeated participation demonstrates a need for one.

A reasonable trigger for a community-format experiment is either:

- At least 100 engaged email subscribers, or
- At least 25 meaningful replies and recurring conversations

At that point, test one small live roundtable or resource workshop before
building a permanent community space.

### Sustainability

Purpose: build a publishing practice, audience, and set of offers that can
continue without exhausting Brent or making the work dependent on a social
algorithm.

Sustainability has three parts:

| Part | Goal |
| --- | --- |
| Creative sustainability | One source idea becomes several appropriate outputs without requiring several unrelated content strategies |
| Relationship sustainability | The website and email list preserve the connection when platforms change |
| Financial sustainability | Useful free work can lead naturally to resources, workshops, speaking, advising, Creative, and the book |

The long-term value path is:

```text
Useful public idea
        ↓
Email relationship
        ↓
Practical free resource
        ↓
Workshop, speaking, advising, or deeper resource
        ↓
Evangelistic Marketing and related teaching
```

The first 90 days should validate usefulness and build trust. They should not be
forced to produce immediate revenue.

## How the Campaigns Work Together

Every source idea should have one primary campaign job and may support the other
two.

| Source action | Resource development | Community building | Sustainability |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publish an essay | Establish a principle that can become a tool | Give readers a shared idea to discuss | Build a durable library |
| Send a Substack edition | Introduce an application or worksheet question | Invite replies and recurring readership | Grow an owned audience |
| Adapt for social | Test which doorway makes the idea understandable | Start conversations with new readers | Create discovery without depending on paid reach |
| Release a beta resource | Turn the principle into practice | Invite readers to test and improve it | Discover future workshop or product demand |
| Develop the book | Unify the strongest frameworks | Give the community a deeper shared argument | Create a durable intellectual and financial asset |

## What to Do With Current Content

Every existing essay gets one of four treatments:

- **Anchor edition:** a full weekly Substack and social cycle
- **Supporting edition:** a shorter edition or an extension to another anchor
- **Resource seed:** material extracted into a worksheet, framework, or tool
- **Hold:** do not distribute until its dependencies are complete

All site essays should receive an editorial readiness check before entering the
weekly distribution cycle. The check should confirm voice, factual support,
theological guardrails, links, visuals, and current terminology.

## Content Plan

The content plan has three simultaneous tracks.

### Source track

Publish or reintroduce one durable idea each week.

For existing essays:

- Keep the permanent site URL and publication date.
- Correct or strengthen the site version before distribution when needed.
- Write a fresh Substack introduction rather than sending an automated copy.
- Select one central claim for the week's social adaptations.
- Connect the essay to a related idea and one useful next step.

For new essays:

- Develop through Content and Blog.
- Add the source to the site only when it meets the editorial standard.
- Record its relationship to *Evangelistic Marketing*.
- Identify the resource or community question it may support.

### Resource track

Build one beta resource during the first 90 days.

Recommended first resource:

> Church Communications Planning Starter Kit

Development sequence:

| Period | Work |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks One through Three | Define the reader, problem, promise, and contents |
| Weeks Four through Six | Build the worksheets from the existing systems essays |
| Weeks Seven through Nine | Test internally and with a small reader group |
| Weeks Ten through Eleven | Revise for clarity and practical use |
| Week Twelve | Release the beta through the site and Substack |

The beta may be a simple PDF or web resource. It does not need a complex
platform.

### Book track

Reserve one focused work block each week for *Evangelistic Marketing* research,
series development, or manuscript planning.

During the first 90 days:

- Create the book content ledger.
- Map current essays to the emerging chapter structure.
- Separate foundational book material from supporting articles.
- Identify research and evidence gaps.
- Continue drafting the ten-part John 4 series as one unified work.
- Do not publish *How People Change* until the series readiness rule is met.
- Record reader questions that reveal where the argument needs more clarity.

## Weekly Social Media Schedule

Each week begins with the anchor idea in the campaign sequence.

| Day | Channel and action | Campaign purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Finalize the Substack introduction, channel adaptations, reply question, and tracking links. No required public post. | Protect quality and creative sustainability |
| Tuesday | Send the Substack edition and direct readers to the permanent site source or related resource. | Build the owned relationship |
| Wednesday | Publish one LinkedIn adaptation focused on a professional or leadership tension. | Discovery and substantive discussion |
| Thursday | Publish one Substack Note with a question, excerpt, diagram, or response to a reader. | Community participation |
| Friday | Publish one Facebook adaptation with personal or ministry context. Review early signals. | Relational conversation |
| Weekend | Use only when the idea benefits from a visual, reflection, or short spoken explanation. | Selective experimentation |

### Monthly social additions

- Two Instagram carousels or diagrams drawn from the strongest visual ideas
- One or two short videos when Brent's speaking voice improves the idea
- One reader-question response that shows the community is shaping the
  conversation
- One older-content extension that connects the current idea to the library

These additions replace optional posts. They do not sit on top of the full
weekly workload.

## The Weekly Content Package

Each anchor idea should normally produce:

- One permanent site source
- One Substack edition with a personal introduction
- One LinkedIn adaptation
- One Facebook adaptation
- One Substack Note
- One reply question
- One resource or book observation recorded internally
- One optional visual or video when the format adds value

Every output has one job and one primary call to action.

## Community Practice

For each edition:

- Ask a question Brent genuinely wants answered.
- Keep the question narrow enough for a reader to answer from experience.
- Reply to substantive responses.
- Record repeated language, objections, and practical examples.
- Ask permission before quoting or identifying a reader publicly.
- Close the loop by showing how a later essay or resource responded to the
  community's input.

Good community questions include:

- Where does this show up on your team?
- What part of this is hardest to explain to your leadership?
- Which touchpoint causes the most confusion in your church?
- What have you tried that worked?
- What would make this framework more useful on Monday morning?

## Resource Beta Group

During Weeks Nine and Ten, invite a small group of roughly eight to twelve
church communicators or leaders to test the Planning Starter Kit.

Ask them to:

- Use at least one worksheet in a real planning conversation.
- Mark anything confusing or unnecessary.
- Identify what is missing.
- Describe what changed in the conversation or decision.
- Give permission separately for any public quotation.

The beta is successful when the resource makes a real decision clearer. The
number of downloads is secondary.

## Sustainability Rules

- Protect one book block each week.
- Keep Monday free of a required public post.
- Do not add a new standing channel without removing or reducing another
  obligation.
- Build no more than one major resource at a time.
- Use existing ideas before demanding more source content.
- Keep the site and email list as the durable foundation.
- Review channel effort monthly and stop work that does not serve discovery,
  relationship, resources, or qualified opportunities.
- Do not introduce a paid membership merely because Substack supports one.
- Introduce a paid resource, workshop, or offering only after repeated reader
  need and a useful solution are both visible.

- Prepare the four Substack editions and their social adaptations.
- Confirm the permanent links, images, and related-content paths.
- Create the *Evangelistic Marketing* content ledger.

- Record meaningful replies and questions.

- Track the time required to produce each weekly package.
- Adjust the cadence if the package repeatedly exceeds the available time.

### Resource development

- Run the small beta.

- Decide which resource or book project receives the next focused development
  cycle.

## Success After 90 Days

The action plan is working when:

- Existing content has been curated rather than buried or bulk reposted.
- At least ten weekly publishing cycles have been completed.
- Readers can repeat the central Brand language accurately.
- Substack is producing recurring replies and returning readers.
- The first practical resource has been tested in real use.
- Reader learning has been added to the book content ledger.
- Brent has protected consistent book development time.
- The workload feels repeatable.
- Qualified opportunities are beginning to emerge without turning the
  publication into a sales channel.

The larger goal is a reinforcing system:

> Ideas become essays. Essays create conversations. Conversations improve
> resources. Resources deepen the community. The community strengthens the
> book. The book, teaching, and related work help sustain the next generation
> of ideas.
