This roadmap turns search-language research into a prioritized set of pages,
essays, resources, and supporting questions for `brentyoung.org`.

The goal is not to publish a large volume of generic SEO copy. The goal is to
help church leaders find clear, useful answers that grow from Brent's lived
experience and existing body of thought. The same work should support
traditional search, AI-generated search answers, social discovery, and the
long-term development of *Evangelistic Marketing*.

## Content Model

Use one durable page for each primary search job. Add a short FAQ section when
related questions can be answered without creating another thin page.

A question deserves its own page when:

- It represents a distinct search intent.
- Brent has a substantive or distinctive answer.
- It requires explanation, examples, evidence, theology, or a framework.
- It can serve as a useful destination for internal links and citations.
- The reader can take a meaningful next step after reading it.

A question belongs inside a page's FAQ when:

- It is a natural follow-up to the primary question.
- A concise answer satisfies the reader.
- A separate page would repeat the parent page or create thin content.

Do not create a generic warehouse of shallow FAQ answers. Google stopped
showing FAQ rich results in May 2026 and removed its FAQ feature documentation
in June 2026. Visible questions can still help readers and search systems, but
FAQ schema is not the strategy.

## SEM and Google Ad Grants Cluster

SEM means search engine marketing. In this roadmap it refers primarily to paid
search advertising, while SEO refers to earning visibility in organic search.
The two should share research, landing-page clarity, measurement, and ethical
guardrails without being treated as interchangeable.

### Current facts that require source-backed treatment

As of 2026-08-15:

- Google describes Ad Grants as up to $10,000 USD per month in in-kind,
  text-based advertising on Google Search for qualifying nonprofits.
- United States applicants must have their own IRS-recognized 501(c)(3) status
  or documented affiliation with a qualifying central organization under a
  group exemption. A church should not assume that automatic tax-exempt status
  alone satisfies Google for Nonprofits verification.
- Google currently uses Goodstack as its nonprofit validation partner.
- The nonprofit must control the advertised domain and maintain a useful,
  functional, secure, mission-focused website.
- Mission-based keyword rules prohibit most single-word and overly generic
  keywords. Keywords with a Quality Score of 1 or 2 must be paused or removed.
- Most Ad Grants accounts must maintain a 5 percent monthly click-through rate.
  Two consecutive months below that threshold can trigger deactivation.
- Active and accurate conversion tracking is required. Meaningful conversions
  can include email signups, volunteer registrations, donations, calls, and
  information requests. Homepage visits and time-on-site should not be treated
  as primary conversions.
- A standard paid Google Ads account may operate alongside an Ad Grants account.
  Google states that the accounts participate in separate auctions.

Program rules change. The page must display a reviewed date, link to Google's
current requirements, and avoid presenting eligibility as legal or tax advice.

## Page Standard

Every primary page should include:

- One clearly stated primary question
- A concise answer near the beginning
- Brent's distinctive reframe or definition
- Firsthand experience, a concrete example, or an original framework
- Useful distinctions that prevent common category errors
- An honest next step the reader can take
- Links to the relevant essay, glossary term, framework, case study, or resource
- A small set of supporting questions
- Accurate publication and modification dates
- Sources for factual, technical, historical, theological, or policy claims
- A descriptive title, meta description, canonical URL, and social preview

Do not force a standard word count. Write until the reader can understand the
question, make a better decision, and stop searching for a more complete answer.

## Traditional and AI Search Standard

Google's current guidance says that ordinary SEO foundations remain the basis
for visibility in generative search. It does not require special AI schema,
tiny content chunks, repeated long-tail variations, or an `llms.txt` file.

Pages should therefore:

- Be crawlable, indexable, fast, accessible, and internally connected.
- Make the main question and answer easy to identify.
- Offer non-commodity, expert-led material rather than summaries of competing
  articles.
- Use consistent entity language for Brent, Church Communications, and
  Evangelistic Marketing.
- Support important claims with primary sources, evidence, and lived examples.
- Use descriptive headings, tables, definitions, and FAQs when they improve the
  reader's understanding.
- Keep policy-sensitive pages current and show when they were reviewed.
- Avoid manufactured mentions, keyword stuffing, and separate pages for every
  trivial variation of a query.

Reference:
[Google's guide to generative AI features in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide).
