# Social Media and Distribution Strategy

Last updated: 2026-07-30

The current execution sequence lives in `PUBLISHING_ACTION_PLAN.md`.

## Purpose

This document defines how brentyoung.org, Substack, email, and social media work
together.

The goal is not to keep several feeds busy.

The goal is to help one durable idea travel clearly, reach the right people,
start a useful conversation, and invite readers into an ongoing relationship
with Brent's work.

The governing idea is:

> The message does not change. The mix does.

Every channel serves the same body of thought, but each channel is shaped for
the people, context, and behavior found there.

## The Communication System

| Layer | Primary role | What belongs there | Primary next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `brentyoung.org` | The permanent home and canonical library | Complete essays, frameworks, glossary terms, case studies, resources, and connections between ideas | Explore a related idea or subscribe |
| Substack publication | The recurring reader relationship | Email editions, personal introductions, editorial notes, replies, discussion, and an accessible path into the larger body of work | Subscribe or reply |
| LinkedIn | Professional conversation and discovery | Observations about leadership, communication, systems, brand, and creative work | Comment, save, share, or subscribe |
| Facebook | Relational conversation with Brent's existing network | Personal context, ministry implications, questions, stories, and links worth discussing | Respond, share with someone, or subscribe |
| Substack Notes | Discovery inside the reader and writer ecosystem | Short observations, excerpts, questions, and responses that extend an edition | Join the discussion or subscribe |
| Instagram | Visual reinforcement, used selectively | Carousels, diagrams, quote graphics, and short visual explanations that genuinely benefit from the format | Save, share, or visit the publication |
| Short video | Optional experiment, not a standing obligation | One clear idea explained in Brent's natural speaking voice | Respond or continue reading |

The website is the library.

Substack is the relationship.

Social media is the conversation.

Email is the return invitation.

*Evangelistic Marketing* is the long-form expression that gathers, tests,
deepens, and unifies the strongest ideas into a book.

None of these replaces the others.

## Source of Truth

Social work normally begins with a durable source idea approved through Content
and Blog.

A source idea may be:

- A published essay
- A finished framework
- A case study
- A glossary definition with enough substance to teach
- A shorter field note intentionally preserved on the site or in the editorial
  system

Social performance may reveal a useful question, objection, phrase, or story.
That learning can return to Content and Blog or Brand and Strategy. It does not
silently rewrite the source article, the First Principles, or the brand
position.

The source content retains final authority over meaning. Channel adaptations
may change the opening, length, example, visual form, and call to action. They
may not change the underlying claim.

## Evangelistic Marketing and the Publishing System

*Evangelistic Marketing: Building a Church Brand That Resonates* is a central
long-term outcome of this body of work.

The book should not be treated as a collection of blog posts pasted into a
manuscript. The public essays are working parts of a larger argument. They help
Brent develop language, test explanations, discover questions, build
frameworks, and earn trust with the future readers of the book.

The relationship is:

```text
Research and lived experience
        ↓
Durable essay or framework
        ↓
Reader questions and ministry application
        ↓
Revised book argument
        ↓
Chapter, visual framework, talk, and resource
```

### What public writing contributes to the book

- Language that makes a complex idea clear
- Real questions and objections from church leaders
- Stories, examples, and applications that reveal where the argument is thin
- Visual frameworks that help readers remember the idea
- Evidence of which ideas deserve a full chapter, a supporting section, or no
  place in the final manuscript
- A growing group of readers who understand the book's central convictions
  before it launches

### What the book contributes to public writing

- A coherent thesis that keeps individual essays from becoming disconnected
- Theological and ethical guardrails
- A sequence that moves from stewardship and story toward trust,
  transformation, advocacy, and community
- A research agenda for claims that require biblical, historical, ministry, or
  marketing support
- A standard for deciding which ideas need greater depth before publication

### Book guardrails

- The book begins with Scripture and faithful ministry, not with a commercial
  framework imposed on the Gospel.
- Communication may prepare a place, remove unnecessary barriers, clarify
  truth, and make a next faithful step visible. It does not manufacture
  transformation.
- People are neighbors to be loved, not leads to be captured.
- Reader response may improve language and application. It does not determine
  theology or rewrite the central thesis by popularity.
- Do not call an essay a book chapter until it has been revised for the larger
  manuscript.
- Do not announce a publication date, preorder, or launch promise until a
  credible manuscript and publishing path exist.

### Book content ledger

Every substantial source idea should record its relationship to the book:

| Field | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Book relationship | Core chapter, supporting section, case study, illustration, framework, or unrelated |
| Central claim | The one claim the material contributes |
| Journey stage | Marketplace, Person, Touchpoint, Pain, Promise, Trust, Truth, Transformation, Advocacy, or Community |
| Evidence needed | Scripture, scholarship, interview, case study, data, or lived example |
| Reader response | Questions, objections, repeated language, and ministry applications |
| Manuscript status | Seed, essay, researched, chapter draft, revised, or final |
| Related output | Visual framework, talk, workshop, podcast, video, or resource |

This ledger belongs with the book's working documents, not inside social
analytics.

## Writing and Marketing the Book

Book development and book marketing should grow together without making every
post promotional.

### Foundation phase

Goal: establish the ideas and the audience.

- Publish foundational essays about stewardship, Brand, story, systems,
  hospitality, trust, and generous invitation.
- Build the Substack relationship.
- Add a quiet, accurate mention of the book in Brent's About page and relevant
  biography.
- Record reader questions and language.
- Develop the John 4 research, theological guardrails, and journey framework.

Primary call to action: join the ongoing conversation.

### Development phase

Goal: develop and test the manuscript's central framework.

- Draft the complete ten-part John 4 series before publishing the first part,
  as required by the series plan.
- Revise the series as one connected argument.
- Publish the series on a consistent cadence once its terminology, theology,
  research, and visual framework are stable.
- Share selected research notes, diagrams, and behind-the-scenes questions when
  they are useful to readers.
- Turn substantive responses into research questions, not testimonials by
  default.

Primary call to action: read, reply, and help identify where the explanation
needs greater clarity.

### Manuscript phase

Goal: turn the body of thought into a unified book.

- Build a chapter map from the thesis rather than from post chronology.
- Identify missing arguments, duplicated ideas, weak transitions, and research
  gaps.
- Rewrite public essays for the manuscript's sequence and voice.
- Develop case studies, visuals, reflection questions, and practical tools.
- Protect focused writing time from the weekly distribution workload.

Primary call to action: remain on the email list for selected progress and
future reader opportunities.

### Prelaunch phase

Goal: gather qualified interest when the book has a credible path to
publication.

- Create a dedicated book page on `brentyoung.org`.
- Open a book-specific interest list or segment.
- Offer a useful sample chapter, framework, or discussion guide.
- Prepare speaking, podcast, workshop, and church-leader materials.
- Invite a small advance reader group with clear expectations.
- Gather endorsements ethically after suitable manuscript material exists.

Primary call to action: join the book list or request a relevant conversation.

### Launch phase

Goal: help the book reach the people it was written to serve.

- Coordinate the website, Substack, email, social, speaking, podcast, and
  partner sequence.
- Give each launch asset a distinct job.
- Equip readers with accurate language, sample material, graphics, and simple
  ways to share.
- Emphasize the reader's problem and the book's useful promise rather than
  Brent's achievement.
- Continue publishing useful ideas during the launch so the relationship does
  not become a prolonged sales campaign.

Primary call to action: purchase, read, discuss, or invite Brent to teach the
material.

### Ongoing book rhythm

Reserve one focused block each week for book research or manuscript
development. Reserve one deeper review block each month to update the chapter
map, content ledger, research gaps, and public publishing sequence.

The weekly social schedule may be reduced when manuscript work requires
concentration. The book is a durable priority. Feed volume is not.

## The Reader Journey

The intended movement is:

```text
Useful social encounter
        ↓
Meaningful conversation or saved idea
        ↓
Substack subscription
        ↓
Repeated email relationship
        ↓
Deeper exploration at brentyoung.org
        ↓
Reply, referral, invitation, resource use, or collaboration
```

Not every reader will move through every step. Every channel post should still
be useful to someone who never clicks.

## Brand Goals

Distribution should steadily help the right readers associate Brent with these
convictions:

- The right story, told the right way.
- Communication is stewardship.
- Design is the intentional process of solving creative problems.
- Brand is the perception people carry, not merely a visual identity.
- Healthy systems create margin for creativity.
- Marketing can be a generous, truthful invitation rather than manipulation.
- Church communication begins caring for people before we know who they are.

The practical public promise remains:

> Helping churches and leaders communicate with clarity, creativity, and
> conviction.

The desired Brand is warm, thoughtful, pastoral, observant, practical,
creative, and trustworthy.

Brent should sound like a teacher across a table, not a commentator performing
for a feed.

## Brand Guardrails

Every adaptation should:

- Preserve the reader's dignity.
- Begin with a real observation, question, tension, or story.
- Teach a principle instead of chasing a trend.
- Use direct, human language.
- Make the idea useful before asking for anything.
- Use the site's canonical vocabulary consistently.
- Extend hospitality through clarity.

Avoid:

- Clickbait, outrage, vague inspiration, and artificial urgency
- Posting only to satisfy a calendar
- Corporate language and thought-leader language
- Advice disconnected from Brent's lived experience or durable body of thought
- Identical copy pasted across every channel
- More than one primary call to action in a post
- Treating reach as proof that an idea is true
- Treating low reach as proof that an idea has no value

## Publishing Cadence

The baseline cadence is one durable source idea each week.

Three weeks each month should normally feature a substantive essay or
framework. The remaining week may feature a shorter field note, case study,
glossary-led explanation, or a deliberate pause for review and development.

Consistency matters more than volume. If the source idea is not ready, the
distribution calendar waits.

### Weekly rhythm

| Day | Work | Public output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Confirm the source idea, audience, central claim, and one desired next step. Prepare channel adaptations. | No required post |
| Tuesday | Publish the canonical source on brentyoung.org. Send the Substack edition with a personal introduction and a clear reply prompt. | Website essay and Substack email |
| Wednesday | Adapt the central tension or story for LinkedIn. Stay present for replies. | One LinkedIn post |
| Thursday | Extend the idea with a short observation, excerpt, or question. | One Substack Note |
| Friday | Adapt the idea relationally for Facebook. Review the week's early signals. | One Facebook post |
| Weekend | Use only when the idea fits the moment naturally. | Optional visual, reflection, or short video |

Instagram is not required every week. Use it when an idea becomes clearer as a
diagram, carousel, visual comparison, or quote worth saving.

Short video is an experiment, not a quota. One useful video every two to four
weeks is enough while the format is being learned.

### Recommended monthly volume

| Channel | Baseline |
| --- | --- |
| brentyoung.org | Three substantive source pieces and up to one shorter field note |
| Substack email | Four editions |
| LinkedIn | Four source adaptations and up to two additional observations |
| Facebook | Four source adaptations |
| Substack Notes | Four to eight notes and thoughtful participation in relevant conversations |
| Instagram | Two to four visual adaptations |
| Short video | One or two experiments |

This is a ceiling for planning, not a minimum required to prove consistency.

## The Two-Week Content Loop

Each source idea can support attention for two weeks without being repeated
verbatim. The extension work overlaps the next weekly publishing cycle. It
replaces optional extra posts rather than adding a second full campaign to the
calendar.

### Publication week

- Publish the complete idea on the site.
- Send the Substack edition.
- Share the story or tension on LinkedIn.
- Ask a genuine question through Substack Notes.
- Share the ministry or personal implication on Facebook.

### Extension week

- Turn the strongest comparison into a carousel or diagram.
- Share one useful excerpt or overlooked implication.
- Answer a real reader question.
- Connect the idea to an older essay, glossary term, framework, or case study.
- Record a short video only if speaking adds something the written version
  cannot.

The extension week gives older work time to travel while the next source idea
is being finished.

## Channel Adaptation Rules

### Website

The website holds the complete and durable version.

- Publish the final title, argument, examples, sources, visuals, and internal
  links.
- Connect the essay to related First Principles, glossary terms, frameworks,
  and case studies.
- Give readers a useful next article, not only a chronological archive.
- Keep `brentyoung.org` as the canonical public source.

### Substack

The Substack edition should feel like correspondence from Brent, not an
automated copy of a web page.

- Add a short personal introduction or note about why the idea matters now.
- Preserve the source argument and Brent's voice.
- Invite one specific kind of reply.
- Link to the permanent site version and related resources when useful.
- Use the sender name `Brent Young`.
- Keep the publication identity unified under Brent's name.

### LinkedIn

LinkedIn is the primary place for professional discovery and discussion.

- Lead with a scene, tension, counterintuitive observation, or practical
  leadership problem.
- Make the post useful on its own.
- Focus on one idea, not a summary of the entire essay.
- Ask a question only when Brent genuinely wants the answer.
- Use a link when the next step adds value. Do not force every post to become a
  link post.

### Facebook

Facebook should sound relational and personal.

- Give more context about why Brent is thinking about the subject.
- Connect the idea to ministry, leadership, creative work, or a lived
  experience.
- Invite stories and experiences rather than debate.
- Use fewer designed assets when plain language feels more natural.

### Substack Notes

Notes should extend participation, not become another broadcast feed.

- Share one observation, question, excerpt, or response at a time.
- Join relevant conversations generously.
- Recommend other writers when their work genuinely serves Brent's readers.
- Avoid turning every Note into a promotion for the latest edition.

### Instagram

Instagram is useful when design improves understanding.

- Prefer diagrams, before-and-after comparisons, annotated examples, and
  carousels that teach one principle.
- Use quote graphics sparingly.
- Do not reduce a nuanced theological or strategic idea to a slogan that
  distorts it.
- Reuse the editorial visual language of the site.

### Short video

Video should preserve Brent's natural speaking voice.

- Explain one idea in roughly 45 to 120 seconds.
- Begin with the problem or observation.
- Speak from a short outline rather than reading polished copy.
- Add captions.
- End with a real question or a simple path to the full idea.

## Calls to Action

Each asset gets one primary next step.

Choose the call to action based on the reader's current relationship:

| Reader state | Best next step |
| --- | --- |
| First encounter | Save, share, or read the full idea |
| Interested reader | Subscribe to Brent Young on Substack |
| Current subscriber | Reply with an experience or question |
| Returning reader | Explore a related essay, framework, or resource |
| Qualified leader or organization | Begin a conversation about speaking, advising, or collaboration |

Useful calls to action sound like invitations:

- What has this looked like on your team?
- Where does this tension show up in your church?
- Reply and tell me what I am missing.
- If this would help someone you lead with, send it to them.
- Read the complete essay and the related framework.
- Subscribe if you want to continue this conversation each week.

Do not stack all of these at the end of one post.

## Ninety-Day Goals

The first 90 days are a launch and learning period.

### Publishing goals

- Complete at least ten weekly publishing cycles.
- Publish at least eight substantive source ideas on brentyoung.org.
- Send at least ten Substack editions.
- Maintain at least 90 percent adherence to the agreed weekly cadence.
- Build a reusable adaptation checklist so each issue does not begin from
  scratch.
- Create the *Evangelistic Marketing* content ledger and map every substantive
  source idea published during the period to the book or mark it intentionally
  unrelated.
- Complete a current chapter map and identify the highest-priority research and
  manuscript gaps.

### Relationship goals

- Gain at least 25 net new email subscribers during the first 90 days.
- Receive at least ten meaningful replies, direct messages, or substantive
  comments across all channels.
- Generate at least three conversations about speaking, advising,
  collaboration, ministry application, or resource use.
- Respond thoughtfully to every substantive reply or comment when practical.

These are starting targets, not forecasts. At the end of the first month,
record the actual baseline and revise the targets if the starting audience or
channel data makes them clearly unrealistic or too easy.

### Performance goals

- Establish a baseline for subscription conversion from the website and social
  channels.
- Reach a Substack click rate of at least 5 percent on editions that contain a
  clear link.
- Average at least three meaningful actions per primary social post. Meaningful
  actions are comments, saves, shares, replies, direct messages, and qualified
  clicks.
- Improve returning traffic to brentyoung.org during the second and third
  months compared with the first-month baseline.

Open rate may be observed, but it is not a primary success measure because mail
privacy features can make it unreliable.

## What to Measure

### Brand signals

- Readers repeat or reference Brent's durable language.
- Comments describe a changed way of seeing the problem.
- People connect Brent with clarity, stewardship, story, design, and systems.
- Readers can describe *Evangelistic Marketing* as generous, truthful,
  mission-shaped invitation rather than church promotion or manipulation.
- Invitations and inquiries fit the people and work the platform is intended
  to serve.

### Relationship signals

- New subscribers
- Replies to editions
- Returning readers
- Direct messages
- Repeat commenters
- Referrals from one reader to another

### Content signals

- Saves and shares with context
- Clicks to the complete source
- Time spent with the source idea
- Related-article exploration
- Questions and objections worth developing

### Reach signals

- Impressions
- Follower growth
- Video views
- Note views

Reach helps diagnose distribution. It is not the final goal.

## Review Rhythm

### Weekly review

Spend roughly 20 minutes recording:

- What was published
- What received meaningful response
- What question or phrase readers repeated
- Which channel adaptation felt natural
- Which adaptation cost more effort than it returned
- What should be answered or tested next

### Monthly review

Review:

- Publishing consistency
- Subscriber sources and growth
- Best-performing source ideas by meaningful action
- Returning site traffic
- Email clicks and replies
- Channel effort compared with value
- Brand language readers are beginning to repeat
- Book questions, objections, research needs, and chapter implications
- Any audience insight that Content or Strategy should consider

### Quarterly review

Decide:

- Which channels remain active
- Which formats deserve more or less effort
- Whether cadence should change
- Which source ideas should become series, frameworks, talks, resources, or
  book material
- Whether any performance insight requires deliberate review by Brand and
  Strategy or Content and Blog

## Distribution Experiments

Run one meaningful experiment at a time for at least four comparable posts.

Good experiments include:

- Story opening compared with question opening
- Text-only LinkedIn post compared with a document carousel
- Direct reply invitation compared with a link invitation
- Tuesday morning compared with Tuesday afternoon email delivery
- Diagram compared with quote graphic
- Full Substack edition compared with a shorter correspondence-led edition

Record:

- The question being tested
- The one variable being changed
- The comparison period
- The result
- The decision to keep, revise, or stop

Do not change voice, theology, or brand conviction merely to improve a metric.

## Tracking Conventions

Use simple campaign parameters when a channel link needs attribution:

```text
utm_source=linkedin|facebook|instagram|substack
utm_medium=social|email
utm_campaign=<source-idea-slug>
utm_content=<format-or-variation>
```

Use lowercase values and the canonical source slug. Do not create a new naming
scheme for every issue.

Maintain a lightweight monthly record with:

- Source title and slug
- Publication date
- Adaptations published
- Subscriber change
- Email clicks and replies
- Meaningful social actions
- Qualified conversations
- Observations and next experiment

The record should support learning, not become a reporting burden.
